Three ways of turning a 10-step robot action chunk into tokens were fine-tuned with an identical LoRA recipe on one LIBERO task and compared by closed-loop success in simulation. With the same steps and the same GPU time, the fixed-plan DWT beat the FAST baseline at every checkpoint from 1000 steps on, and adaptive DWT overtook FAST from 1500 steps on. The FAST baseline peaked at 1000 steps and then declined, while almost all of its generated streams were malformed (they did not decode to exactly 70 DCT coefficients).

3. Training curves (learning-curve runs, seeds 0–2)

The step-like drops in training loss sit at multiples of 365 steps, one pass over the 5840 training windows at batch 16 (365, 730, 1095, 1460, 1825): each new epoch revisits windows already seen, a sign of memorization. Seeds are almost indistinguishable in training loss.

Held-out token loss rises after roughly 1000 steps for every variant: with 40 demonstrations the adapters start to memorize them. It does not predict closed-loop success (next section), and losses are not comparable across variants because the vocabularies and stream lengths differ.

Latency is autoregressive generation of one chunk with a KV cache on the A6000. DWT streams are about twice as long as FAST streams, so latency roughly doubles; at 10 executed actions per chunk this is still within LIBERO's simulated control loop but would need speculative or parallel decoding for real-time control at higher rates.

7. Wavelet choice (offline selection test)

All trained models use Haar (db1). 8. Caveats

  • One task and one instruction. Nothing here says how the tokenizers behave in multi-task training or on other robots.
  • 20 episodes per checkpoint and 3 seeds: confidence intervals are wide, and the paired tests are not corrected for multiple comparisons.
  • The FAST baseline learns LIBERO FAST tokens through the same small LoRA budget. Its low value-token accuracy and malformed streams suggest part of the gap is how hard BPE tokens are to adapt under this recipe; the fully fine-tuned reference shows FAST itself can solve the task.
  • Training is not bit-reproducible: rerunning seed 0 did not exactly reproduce the pilot because of nondeterministic GPU kernels.
  • Models are LoRA fine-tunes, not full fine-tunes; the base model stays frozen.
